We praise the canopy,  
the violent bloom,  
the heavy fruit that bends the summer branch.  
We worship what the eye can calculate.  

Yet the whole miracle begins in blindness.  

A seed is not asleep;  
it is submitting to the crushing dark.  
Surrounded by wet soil,  
deprived of sky,  
it leaves behind the safety of its shell  
not knowing what a tree is.  

It yields its shape because it must.  
It grows downwards first,  
gripping the rock, drink from the cold,  
building invisible anchors in the deep.  

Honor the season where nothing can be seen.  
The soil is not your tomb;  
it is your womb.  
Be still.  
Let the husk break.
